# Prioritisation rubric

**The rule.** Score every candidate from the braindump on five axes before ranking any of them, not only the ones that are top of mind. An item whose revenue type is "none" ranks below any item with a `£` tag, however vivid the signal behind it.

| Axis | Question | Example row |
|---|---|---|
| Goal anchor | Which objective in the current OKRs does it serve? If none, it must earn its place another way. | O1 |
| Revenue type | New / protected / cash collection / amplification / none | New |
| Quantified £ | A rough figure, forced even when rough | £X |
| Time to impact | This week / this month / compounding (three months plus) | This month |
| Reversibility | Can it be dropped without harm? High means drop candidate, low means must ship. | Low |

**The incident.** In the week of 25–31 May 2026 the retro ranked from memory, and the items that felt urgent were the ones mentioned most often on calls. The week produced roughly twenty-four thousand lines of code and skipped the one item with a real pipeline behind it, exactly as the previous retro had predicted in writing.

Cost class: a wasted week, foreseen and not prevented.

**How the skills enforce it.** `weekly-retro` will not produce a ranked list until every braindump item has a scored row. The rubric table is in the weekly template. Frequency of mention is not an axis.

If you're in a team: score independently, then compare. Agreeing on axes is easier than agreeing on rank.
