# Prioritisation pitfalls

**The rule.** Read this checklist before locking a ranking. Each item is a mistake made in a real retro, and the rubric exists to stop it recurring.

1. **Frequency of mention is not revenue impact.** "Several people asked for it" is vivid, not a `£` signal.
2. **A paying customer asking for X does not mean X drives revenue.** They have already paid. New revenue and recovered cash beat incremental satisfaction.
3. **Recency bias.** The latest calls overwhelm the OKR weighting. Recheck against the objectives before ranking.
4. **Keystone is not top impact.** Keystone means leverage over months. Top impact means this week. Rank them separately.
5. **Unquantified compounding.** "Becomes durable collateral" sounds big. Do the rough maths before treating it as decisive.
6. **Your own narrative.** A story you just built makes everything inside it feel important. Force-rank against items outside it.
7. **A task that keeps slipping is an overcomplication signal.** Suspect the mental model, not the priority. Look for the isolate-it-entirely shortcut first.
8. **Under people-load, the builder defaults to building.** The category that slips is always sell, close, comms, hire. Delivery and tooling always ship.

**The incident.** Pitfall 7 came from a small onboarding task that sat on the list for weeks, imagined as integration work across three systems. It turned out to be a fifteen-minute job done in isolation. An external person chased several times while it waited.

Cost class: a missed lead, through reputational bleed rather than lost work.

**How the skills enforce it.** `weekly-retro` prints the eight as a checklist beside the ranked list and asks the human to tick each one. Pitfall 8 feeds the `protect` line in every standup.
