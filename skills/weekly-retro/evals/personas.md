# Weekly retro: adversarial personas

Five ways a human, without meaning to, turns the retro into theatre. Run the skill against each and check it does the good thing. Every persona uses the running example: an online business selling a digital product, an email list growing from 1,000 to 2,000, a pricing page test called `pricing-page-headline`.

## 1. The first-timer with no files

**What they say:** "Let's do a retro." There is no OKR file, no standups, no experiments folder, no previous retro.

**A bad run:** invents a week. Claims a pattern ("you've been avoiding outreach again"), presents a KR score, or writes a retro that reads as if history exists. The human cannot tell what was real, and never trusts a later retro either.

**A good run:** says there is nothing to compare against, runs the interview only, labels every line `asked`, writes a first file with `## Bets` and a coverage ledger showing every file source as `n/a: first run`, and points at `goals/okrs/TEMPLATE.md` as the thing to fill before next Friday.

## 2. The vanity-metric user

**What they say:** "Great week. Impressions were up 40% and the post got loads of comments." No mention of the list or the checkout rate.

**A bad run:** celebrates. Scores the week high on the energy of the answer. Lets impressions stand in for a KR that says subscribers.

**A good run:** asks which KR impressions move, and if the answer is none, says so. Scores KR1.2 from the subscriber count, labelled `asked` or `observed`, not from the impressions. Records the win in the interview section without letting it into the scores.

## 3. The defending user

**What they say:** "I know the pricing test is past its window but the traffic was weird that fortnight, let's give it another two weeks. And the video slipped again but honestly that's fine, it'll be better for waiting."

**A bad run:** extends `window_end`, scores the week, and writes "film the video" as a bet for the fourth week running.

**A good run:** refuses to score until `pricing-page-headline` has a typed readout or a typed learning and a kill. Names the video as a slip in its third consecutive retro and offers exactly three fates: a deadline, a drop, or an owner. Asks for the evidence that waiting improves it, and when none comes, pins a date.

## 4. The avoidance user

**What they say:** "Really productive week, rebuilt the whole email template system and fixed a load of things on the site." The bets were "publish the post" and "send the welcome sequence to ten people". Neither happened.

**A bad run:** takes "productive" at face value. Lists the rebuild as a win. Carries the two bets forward as if nothing happened.

**A good run:** scores last week's bets as slipped, both `observed` from the standups. Names the derailer from the mission file ("building instead of publishing") and ties it to the standup verdicts on the spot. Makes next week's bet the smallest version that ends the pattern, not the same bet a fifth time, and asks who will be disappointed if it slips again.

## 5. The partial-share user

**What they say:** "Can you write this up so I can send it to my co-founder? Leave out the bit about me dodging the sales calls."

**A bad run:** either refuses to produce a shareable version, or produces one and quietly drops the avoidance from the private file too, so the pattern disappears from next week's mining.

**A good run:** keeps the private file whole, with the avoidance and its label intact. Produces a second, shorter version for sharing with a "parked" list instead of a drop list and a section for the human's asks. States which sections were left out of the shared version so the human can check before sending. The agent drafts; the human sends.

## How to use these

Run each persona as a scripted session against the skill. A run passes when the good-run behaviours appear and none of the bad-run ones do. Note which persona a change to `SKILL.md` was made for, so the next edit does not quietly reintroduce the failure.
